Data & methodology

Source
Trendlyne. Exports refresh monthly; an export date is not a guarantee of fresh source data for every index. Per-index indicators distinguish successful refreshes, cached data after a failed attempt, overdue refreshes and unknown dates. Older workbooks show unknown until a refresh is recorded.
Returns
Calendar-year, quarterly and monthly index returns reported by Trendlyne. Monthly and quarterly histories are fetched together, with independent refresh status and saved-data fallback. Only completed months and quarters are shown. The current year's annual value is YTD, not a completed year. Blank values mean unavailable or unfinished periods. Monthly mode does not change the quarterly-based CAGR, growth or risk calculations.
Return basis
The source's price-return versus total-return designation has not been independently verified. The export and Research views label it unverified rather than claiming dividend-inclusive TRI data. No dividends are added separately. Index histories are not returns of an investable fund and should not be mixed with verified TRI series without checking their basis.
Data quality
Coverage spans the first through last valid monthly observation, with missing months counted inside that span. This does not prove a complete inception year. Non-finite export values become blanks; returns below -100% and duplicate monthly observations are flagged. Ambiguous duplicate months are excluded from Research calculations. For each quarter with three valid completed months and a reported quarterly value, compounded monthly returns are compared with that value. A difference above 0.10 percentage points is flagged for review, not automatically corrected. This tolerance is a rounding-aware screening threshold, not proof of source accuracy.
Growth of ₹10,000
Quarterly compounding on the latest uninterrupted history shared by all selected indices. Each line starts at ₹10,000 immediately before the first included quarter. Only completed quarters are included; the result can differ from the latest annual YTD value. Changing the selection or range resets the common starting date.
Assumptions
Hypothetical index growth, not an investable portfolio. Asset and Compare growth exclude fees, taxes and additional cash flows; dividends are not added separately. The Research portfolio simulator includes the explicitly selected trading cost, as described below.
CAGR
Stat tiles use the last 4 × N available consecutive quarterly returns. Historical table values compound N calendar years; current-year CAGR ends at the last available completed quarter.
Comparison metrics
Excess return is the annual index return minus Nifty50's same-year return, in percentage points. Rolling 3Y and 5Y CAGR use 12 and 20 consecutive completed quarters at each quarter-end; a missing quarter leaves a blank. Year filters select endpoints, not the lookback history. Curved lines interpolate between observations; they are not extra data points.
Drawdown and recovery
Quarter-end drawdown is the decline from the highest compounded value since the common starting date. Recovery counts quarters below that peak, resetting to zero once it is regained. The risk table reports the worst observed episode's peak-to-recovery duration, or marks it unrecovered. Daily and within-quarter losses are not measured; changing the range or selected indices resets the shared history and peak.
Statistics and filters
Best/worst year, up years and annual volatility use completed years across the full available history. Stat tiles are not changed by the chart/table year filters. Market Overview rankings and the annual chart use all available data; monthly and quarterly heatmaps show the selected calendar year. Research uses independent month controls, defaulting to the latest 60 months.
Benchmark screener
Each 12-, 36- or 60-month window requires consecutive completed monthly returns for both the index and Nifty50. Outperformance is the index's compounded total window return minus Nifty50's in percentage points, not an annualized excess return. Win rate is the fraction of matched windows with strictly positive excess; ties are not wins. The minimum matched-window filter defaults to 12. Selected dates filter window endpoints, not lookback history. Latest excess is blank if the index cannot match the latest benchmark endpoint, rather than substituting an older result. Overlapping windows are dependent observations, not probabilities of future success. Drawdown uses each index's latest uninterrupted shared history with Nifty50 inside the selected dates; its months and dates may differ across rows.
Correlations
Pearson sample correlation of reported monthly returns on one common, uninterrupted history for all positively weighted indices. At least 24 shared monthly observations are required; zero-variance or uncomputable correlations remain blank, including the diagonal. Values near +1 indicate similar movements, near -1 opposite movements; neither is a return or a prediction. Changing weights, selection or dates can change the common sample. Statistics use the bundled simple-statistics library (ISC license).
Portfolio simulation
Non-negative target weights must total 100%; zero-weight indices do not constrain history. Simulation starts with the selected initial investment immediately before the first month of the latest uninterrupted shared history inside the selected dates. Initial purchases incur the selected cost in basis points per amount purchased. Holdings then drift with reported monthly returns. Rebalancing occurs after returns at calendar month, quarter or year ends, except at the final observation. The fee is the selected rate times actual buys plus sells; target holdings are solved after accounting for that fee. Buy-and-hold never rebalances. Fractional holdings and immediate month-end execution are assumed. There are no additional cash flows, taxes, fund tracking differences, separately added dividends or final liquidation. Nifty50 buy-and-hold uses the same starting amount and initial purchase cost, only when its full matching history is available.
Portfolio risk
CAGR annualizes the final net value relative to the initial investment over the actual number of simulated months and requires at least 12 months. Annualized volatility is the sample standard deviation of realized net monthly portfolio returns multiplied by the square root of 12; it requires 24 return observations and includes modeled costs. Max drawdown uses month-end portfolio values, including the initial balance; intra-month losses are not measured. Correlations are calculated on source index returns, not the fee-adjusted portfolio. PNG/CSV exports include the simulation settings.
Calculators
SIP, LumpSum and SWP are hypothetical constant-return cash-flow projections, independent of index history. The input is an effective annual return; the monthly rate is (1 + annual return / 100) raised to 1/12, minus 1. The bundled financial 0.2.4 library supplies future-value math (MIT license). SIP deposits occur at the selected month start or month end. LumpSum invests once at the start. SWP earns monthly growth before month-end withdrawals, which are capped at available funds; the first depletion month and cumulative requested-withdrawal shortfall are reported. Annual SIP/withdrawal increases apply after each 12 projection months. The graph includes the starting balance and month-end values, with zero balances retained after SWP depletion. Gains equal remaining balance plus actual withdrawals minus invested capital. Yearly rows represent completed 12-month projection years. Fees, taxes and inflation are excluded; these assumed returns are not guaranteed. PNG captions and CSVs preserve input assumptions. Supported duration is 1–50 years, annual return -50% to 50%, and annual increase 0% to 50%.
Risk
Market Overview annual volatility is sample standard deviation of calendar-year returns. Its risk-adjusted score is mean annual return divided by annual volatility, not a Sharpe ratio. Past performance does not predict future returns; this is not investment advice.
